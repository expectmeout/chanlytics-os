# 0120 · Cold email runs itself

**Status:** Accepted by Ruben, 9 November 2026 ("Yes, plan it" for calls D4 to D8 of the canvas, then "continue end to end until stage 3 complete"), and built on his word of the same day. The third of five stages of "one timeline per person" (the plan is outside this repository, `docs/2026-11-09-sequences-timeline-build-plan.md`). Written against the `cold-email` branch at `0aefc69eed` (main `8a71edf1a3` with pull request 2214, decision 0116), and moved onto main `f6cbac48b2` once that pull request merged. Its nine parts are built; what each leaves out is named below.

## Context

Decision 0116 made a mailbox a requirement and gave cold email its page. What it left is how much of the owner's day cold email still takes:

- **It never sends on its own.** A sequence to leads holds each batch thirty minutes or asks for each (`sequenceModesFor("leads")`, and `change_playbook` refused `auto` for a reach-out's step, 00462). An owner who has watched a sequence go for a month cannot let it go.
- **One waiting OK froze the whole sequence.** While any run of a chain waited, nothing more was made (`chainWaits` in core `run-reach-out.ts`): not new first emails, and not the follow-ups to businesses already written to, who were left mid-conversation for as long as the owner did not answer.
- **A run that waits was told once.** A run to leads has no day to pass (wait rule `leads_batch`, `ends: "never"`). Its one notice could sit unread for weeks, with the sequence stopped behind it.
- **Ask's card asked twice.** Its cold email card showed each email's subject and said the first run "waits for your say": a press on the card, then an OK of the run, for words the owner had just approved.
- **Nothing said what went.** "Since you looked" counted what the receptionist answered. A sequence that sends on its own would have left no line anywhere on Today.

## Decision

1. **A sequence to leads may send on its own, where its owner sets that on the sequence** (D4). `sequenceModesFor` answers all three modes for a sequence that exists, and `change_playbook` no longer refuses `auto` for a reach-out's step (00599). A new sequence still starts held or asked: `sequenceStartModesFor("leads")` is what New and the Cold email page's row for new sequences offer, and `sequence_mode_for` and `sequence_create_leads` are unchanged, so nothing sends on its own that its owner did not set to. New words still wait for their first OK whatever the mode (`batchModeOf`). Under `auto` the run starts at once, with no thirty minutes and no "it goes at" bell, and is recorded as the system's send (core `runReachOut`).
2. **A run that waits holds what is behind it, and no more** (D5). The port is `waitingSteps(rootId)`: the steps with a run waiting for approval. While any waits, the first step plans nothing, so no new business is written to. A later step is held only while a run of its own waits, so under Ask me they never pile up, and a first email that waits never holds the follow-ups to the people already written to.
3. **While a run to leads waits, its owner is reminded each morning** (D5). The hourly playbooks sweep asks `remindColdEmailWaits` after its runs: for each workspace with a run that has waited twelve hours or more, between nine and noon by its own clock (contracts `coldWaitsReminderOf`), one line on the bell and the phones: how many emails wait, in which sequences, since when. Kept once a workspace a day, under a name made of the workspace and its day. No mail: Settings promises no digest. The row opens the longest-waiting run and closes with it. The wait's own sentence says so (`waitEndsWords("leads_batch")`, a rule that `reminds`).
4. **The press of Ask's cold email card is the OK of its words.** The card now shows every email whole, its subject and its words (`cold-email-card.ts`), and says what its press does. Its payload says the words were shown (`words_shown`), the commit carries that to `POST reach-outs` (contracts `ReachOutMakeSchema`), and where it came through Ask the door marks each step's words as seen (`words_seen`, 00573) before it asks for the first run. So the first run goes as Before each batch says, and the receipt says so. Any other make, and a database that could not mark a step, leaves the first run waiting as before. This is decision D3 of the answers canvas, which held for a customer's sequence, reaching cold email.
5. **"Since you looked" says what the sequences sent** (D8). Today's read carries `since.sent`: each sequence that sent since the member last looked, how many of its messages left and when the last did, counted off the sends that left (db `listSentSince`, over the workspace's newest forty batches that are going or gone). A sequence is named as its newest batch is and is to leads where that batch is a cold email's, so the read is of batches and their sends alone, which Today already hears. The line says one bit for them all ("12 sent") and, opened, a row for each sequence, the latest first, opening that sequence.
6. **Cold email is one row of what waits** (D8). The one list of the runs that wait (`waitRowsOf`, the dashboard's `surfaces/waits/rows.ts`) folds two or more of cold email's runs that wait, for an OK or with emails its check holds for a look, into one row: "Cold email", how many sequences, what is held for a look and how many emails an OK would send. It opens the run holding emails first, then those waiting for an OK, and a run's screen walks the runs of its own kind ("1 of 3"), so the walk ends where the row does. "N runs wait for your OK" is now a customer campaign's alone, and one cold email run alone is its own row as before. The emails handed to their owner stay To send's. Today's count, the badge and the spoken brief read the same rows, so each says cold email once.
7. **"Not now" and "wrong person" arrive written** (D7). The reading's plan says when the step it leaves arrives with its note (contracts `readingPlan`'s `ahead`): a not-now's check-back the morning its step is due (9:00 by the business's clock, the step's own day), and a first note to the person a wrong person named, written at once, where they named an address (`addressNamedIn`: not the one we wrote to, nor the one that answered). The reply's own job hands it to a stage of its own (`ahead`, core `readReply`), which writes it only while it is still owed (db `findAheadOwed`: the step open, nothing newer from them on the thread, the lead still there and not closed), through the receptionist's draft writer with a note of its own kind (`leadReplyNote`: `again`, `referral`), and keeps it on their thread as a draft of kind `step` (00600), naming the step and whom it is to. Its edition is its own (`STEP_NOTE_RULES`), so a page a deploy behind reads none of it as the thread's reply. On Today, a to-do that arrives with its note opens to read it ("Read the note") instead of marking itself done, and its sheet opens with the words written: a not-now's Send sends it on their thread and finishes the to-do, as the step sheet's Send always did (his call D10); a note to someone new opens in the owner's own mail (`mailtoOf`), and Done finishes it. A note the receptionist would not write (no receptionist, drafts off, the day's budget) leaves the to-do as it always was.
8. **A business that never answered may be tried again after 90 days** (D6), off unless its owner turns it on, on How it runs (`POST sequences/:id/again`, 00601 `reach_out_again_set`, a member who may approve). One more first email, its line about them written again from their listing as it is then, to a business whose every email is that old, none on its way, none bounced, none answered and nothing they wrote; never to anyone who said no, became a customer (anyone in People but the lead's own person holding the address), was stopped by their owner, or shares with another lead (`reach_out_comes_back`, which asks each of those reasons again, since a written lead reads to `reach_out_why_not` as closed or a customer as often as written). The audience gives them after the new businesses (`reach_out_audience`'s `p_again_after`, named only when set). Once, never a third: a send keeps its round (`sequence_sends.round`, set by `reach_out_plan_run`), a business tried again never comes back, and no follow-up goes after it (`reach_out_followers` follows a first round's email alone). The rule of one first email a lead is now one a lead a round (00602 builds `sequence_sends_one_first_a_round`, 00603 drops the old, each alone, concurrently); until both run, a second first email is refused at the row and nothing is written. Turned on, the sequence's words wait for an OK once more (its first step's `words_ok_at` cleared), as any new words do. The switch says who would come back and from when (`reach_out_again_count`: "8 businesses would come back in February").

## What it does not do

- **A held email is still answered on its run, not in Today's sheet.** The canvas walks each held email in Today's own sheet (Skip them, Write another, Send anyway). The row here opens the run, where each held email is put back or skipped as before; after the last of a run's held emails, the owner goes back to Today for the next run.
- **A note that came due is a to-do, not one of Need you's rows.** The canvas has it under "Waiting on you"; here it stays in To do, where its step always stood, with "Read the note" and the words written. Need you's count does not include it.
- **A note to the person a wrong person named goes from the owner's own mail.** Chanlytics' send door does not write to a business that has written back, and a road around it for a new address is a change to the one send door, left for its own decision. Nobody is added to People for them: an address in People would read as a customer's to every sequence after.
- **The notes have no evals of their own yet.** They are the receptionist's draft writer with a line of their own (`leadReplyNote`); the words a model writes for a check-back or a first note to someone new have not been read over a set of real replies.
- **Trying again sends the first email only.** A business tried again gets no follow-ups; a sequence that wants more says so later.
- **The Cold email page has no row for trying again** (task 3.8): it is a sequence's own, on its How it runs, as Before each batch is.
- **Under `auto` nobody is told as a batch goes.** The record and the "Since" line say what went. An owner who wants a word before each batch keeps Hold it.
- **The reminder reads the five hundred longest-waiting runs a sweep.** A workspace behind more than that is reminded once those ahead are answered.
- **Not walked in the app whole, and the database suites have not run.** The node tests of what changed, CI's own selection of them and the typecheck ran here, and the Cold email row was looked at through a rig; the replay of 00599-00603 and every `.db.test.mjs` (among them `tests/reach-out/alone/tried-again.db.test.mjs`) are CI's.

## Deploy order

00599 and 00600 either way: a build before 00599 still offers two choices; a build after it against a database before it gets the old refusal in the door's own words, and nothing is written; before 00600 a step's note is refused at its keep and the to-do stands as it was. **00601 before the build**: the build asks `reach_out_audience` with `p_again_after` only where a sequence is set to try again, which only 00601's door can set, but reads `sequence_steps.again_after_days` with every step. 00602 after 00601, and 00603 after 00602, each by hand (an index built or dropped concurrently): until 00603 runs, a business tried again is refused at the row, and nothing is written.
