# Reports phase C, lens 4: the Campaigns lens, drawn from what is stored (C4) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** The Campaigns lens answers "did my messages reach people, did they answer, and what is stuck?" in the lenses spec's five parts (D7: sentence, against before, mix, time between steps, stuck now), from reads of its own: the funnel and the per-campaign rows from the report the Campaigns home already reads (one number on two pages), the people, the held and the failed, the replies by message and the waits from one new window function, what is stuck now from one new as-of-now function, and the reach-outs counted in businesses. A yes or a stop on this lens is an answer to a campaign, on the Home too.

**Architecture:** Three reads, one surface, one fold. The window: SQL `campaign_series` (its consent part redefined by migration 00397 to the run sheet's rule, held equal to it by a database test) and `campaign_ledger` (00399, new: people, runs, held, why sends failed, replies by message, send to first reply, wait for your OK); as of now: `campaign_stuck` (00400, new: raw facts, judged in contracts by the product's own rules); reach-outs: `reach_out_report` and `reach_out_chain_totals` (00398, redefined: businesses over businesses, and the period before for what was written). The ledger and the stuck facts are read together by `GET /api/v1/reports/campaigns/ledger` (reader `campaignLedgerReport`, contracts DTO `CampaignLedgerDto`); the surface `reports_campaigns` (a `periodSurface`) reads that, the existing `GET reports/campaigns` and the existing `GET reports/reach-outs` (its `report` half), each failing alone, and publishes `{ campaigns, ledger, reachOuts, key }`. The page folds them once with `toCampaignLensVM` and `toReachOutLensVM` from the page-only entry `@chanlytics/contracts/report-lens`. The frame stops reading campaigns and reach-outs; the Overview's "drew the most replies" fact is made on the page from the same surface.

**Tech Stack:** Postgres/plpgsql on Supabase (SECURITY INVOKER functions, two SECURITY DEFINER ones re-created), TypeScript (`packages/contracts`, `packages/db`, Next.js App Router dashboard), React with the repo's kit and hand-rolled SVG charts, `node:test` (one file per command), Playwright for `e2e/`.

## The design's rulings this plan carries (R1-R13, decided, not reopened)

- **R1** The funnel is one number on two pages. Sent, delivered, read (WhatsApp's own), wrote back, by channel, and the per-campaign and broadcast rows keep coming from `campaign_series` and its existing fold `toCampaignReportVM`, which the Campaigns home reads too. The lens draws them now (defect b), with D9's n and minN on the ratios.
- **R2** New window parts come from one new read, `campaign_ledger` (INVOKER, the 00395 window, counts only): (a) people sent to and people who wrote back (the 72 h window, a person's own words, any channel), now and before, and runs by kind (moment, broadcast; reach-out runs are not counted here); (b) held and failed recipients by reason, dated by their run's time (trap 1), and runs canceled or failed by `failure` code; (c) why sends failed, by `{channel, provider code}` from `messages.status/error` joined by the recipient's `message_id` (trap 3; code and channel only); (d) per `(template_name, template_language)` the sends, the mature sends and the replies (traps 5, 11); (e) send to first reply (bucketed, median with n); (f) wait for your OK per approved run (`decisions` verb `send_broadcast`, `payload->>'approved' = 'true'`; trap 9).

## Review Focus

The five conditions the design implies and no task's happy-path test would meet, most likely first. Each has its test in the task named.

1. **One person, several sends, several answers** (the double count): a person on two runs, answering twice, or answering after both. The head counts them once as a person, the funnel counts both sends, a yes or a stop counts once a period, and a person who said yes and then stopped within one run's window counts as the stop (the run's latest). Test: Task 2 (answers, once a person-answer-period), Task 5 (people distinct, sends not), Task 12 (the head says people, the funnel messages).
2. **A reply still coming** (maturity): a window that ends today holds sends under 72 hours old that have had no time to be answered, so a plain comparison reads as a fall. The ledger counts them (`counting`), the by-message rate divides by mature sends only, and the figure and the card say so. A window that ended is complete. Test: Task 5 (`counting`, `mature`, a window that ended), Task 12 (the words), Task 14 (the card).
3. **A workspace with nothing sent, or only reach-outs** (the quiet period, defect c): one quiet card, never a wall of empty ones; the head says "Nothing went out in this period" and what the reach-outs wrote; Stuck now and Reach-outs still draw; no `NaN`, no 0%. Test: Task 12 (all-zero facts), Task 14 (the page), Task 15 (reach-outs alone).
4. **One read missing or failed** (each fails alone): a database without `campaign_ledger` (00399) or `campaign_stuck` (00400) draws the funnel, the channels, the sources and the days and no failure; a read that failed says what failed and what still stands. Test: Task 8 (null), Task 10 (reader), Task 12 (the fold), Task 14 (the page), Task 16 (Ask).
5. **Free text, other people's rows and the edges of a day** (what must never leave): a `reason` with a sentence in it, an `error.body` holding a phone number, a recipient of another workspace, an erased or merged person, a send at 23:50 local east of UTC. Test: Task 5 (sanitised keys, the Auckland day, `unknown_tenant`), Task 2 (live only, a member of another workspace reads none), Task 6 (a caller who is no member).

Also pinned where they belong: a held person is dated by their run and a failed message by its own time (Task 5); a reach-out run is never a campaign run (Task 5, Task 6); a deleted playbook's run keeps its kind from `audience` (Task 5); an email unsubscribe days later is the answer to the email it names and to no other run (Tasks 1 and 2); a template used by no run still shows when Meta stopped it (Task 6); the Home's yes and stop line and its chain cell say what the lens says (Tasks 2, 4); `reach_out_chain_totals` equals the sums of the report's steps for a listed chain (Task 3); the superset the replies prefilter guarantees over `standingOfThread` is held by a matrix (Task 6).

## Where the code differs from the design, and what this plan does

1. **R4: `consentAfter` is private, async and reads through a client, and it is not the rule R4 describes.** It lives in `apps/dashboard/src/server/campaigns/broadcasts.ts` (line 440), reads events and recipients through the session client, counts a merge's restated answer, has no live-person test, and has no exact links. R4 wants those three and wants the SQL equal to it. Ruling: the rule becomes one pure contracts function, `answersToRuns` (`packages/contracts/src/broadcasts/run-answers.ts`, Task 1), which `consentAfter` calls (so the run sheet gains the three rules R4 names, and the PR says so), and the SQL `campaign_answers` (Task 2) is held to it by a database test that reads one world's rows and runs both. A rule R4 leaves open is fixed here: an event that carries an exact key (an email unsubscribe, a complaint or a "reply no" with the outbound email's `message_id`; a carrier or WhatsApp stop with the refused send's `message_external_id`) is attributed by its key alone, to that send's run, however late it came, and to no run when the key finds no campaign send (it answers something else); every other event is attributed by time.
2. **R4's unit.** "The latest answer per person per run" decides which answers are the campaigns', but counting it per run would count a person reached by two runs twice and could exceed `people_series.consent` (one per person, kind and period), which R4 also wants as a ceiling. Ruling: `campaign_series` counts each person once per answer and period, on the day of their earliest such answer (as `people_series` does), so the fold's sums are people, the metric words say "once in the period" (not "once a day each"), and the subset test holds. The Home's yes and stop line drops to this count; the PR says so.
3. **R3: the number's health is not read from SQL.** `campaign_stuck` would restate which `tenant_channels` row is "the number" (`whatsappChannelFacts` picks the oldest active WhatsApp channel) and would carry `config` (which holds credentials-adjacent keys) toward a DTO. `whatsappHealthOf` (`apps/dashboard/src/server/campaigns/channels-ready.ts`) already derives `numberHealth` + `numberHealthWithStanding` from that row. Ruling: `campaign_stuck` returns no config; the reader calls `whatsappChannelFacts` and a new pure `numberHealthOfConfig(config)` (in `packages/contracts/src/lines/number-standing.ts`, Task 9) that `whatsappHealthOf` is changed to call too, and puts only named, derived keys and the read times in the DTO (`CampaignNumberDto`). The read times (`quality_read_at`, `standing_read_at`, `quality_at`) are from the same config; the gate's sources, never `quality_read` alone.
4. **R6 and the Overview.** The frame (`surface.ts`) builds `reportFacts(vm, campaigns)` at apply time and the Overview reads `R.facts`; with campaigns out of the frame that fact needs the new surface. Ruling: `ReportsSurface` loses `campaigns`, `reachOuts` and `facts`; the page makes the facts (`reportFacts(R.vm, campaignsVM)`, whose second parameter narrows to `Pick<CampaignReportVM, "sources">`) from the `reports_campaigns` follower, so the fact appears when that read lands (accepted, as C3's head changes once). `lensAnswerOf`'s `campaigns` part becomes `{ head: string | null } | null`, as People's. Because `ROUTE_DRAWS["reports"]` also answers a lens with no entry of its own (`routeDrawsFor` falls back to the page), `"reports/pipeline": []` is added so the Pipeline lens reads nothing it does not draw. The Overview therefore reads the lens's three reads to say one fact (Open question 2).
5. **R7 and the Reach-outs fold.** `toReachOutReportVM` and its words (`reach-out-report.ts`) are in the root entry and only Reports and Ask read them; the units and the period before change nearly every word of them. Ruling: the fold moves page-only as `toReachOutLensVM` (`reports/campaigns/reach-out-lens.ts`, Task 11) and the old fold and its view-model types leave `reach-out-report.ts`, which keeps the DTOs, `chainTotalsOf`, `chainTotalsFromReport` and `reachOutDayWords`; the route's JS falls. Tests that call the old fold move with it (`tooling/scripts/tests/reach-out-back/report-words.test.mjs` keeps its 6 PM and chain-totals cases).
6. **R12: the lead.** A run's recipients send after the run is made: up to the 30 days a broadcast may be scheduled ahead (`BROADCAST_SCHEDULE_MAX_DAYS`) plus the holds and throttles. Ruling: SQL says it once, as `public.campaign_run_lead()` (37 days, as `campaign_reply_window()` says the 72 hours), a database test holds it to `BROADCAST_SCHEDULE_MAX_DAYS + 7`, and the window is driven from `broadcasts_tenant_created (tenant_id, created_at)` with `created_at >= the period before's first instant - lead and < the window's stop`, then the recipients of those runs by their primary key. `campaign_series`' own `sent` scan (every recipient of the workspace) is left as it is unless Task 7 measures it past its gate (Open question 7).
7. **Trap 7, the provider code: one exported parser, as the design says.** `providerCodeOf` is module-private in `conversations/message-words.ts`. Ruling: Task 5 exports it (an `export`, one barrel name in `conversations/index.ts`; the folder is at its 20 modules and takes no file) and the database test calls it directly against `public.provider_code_of` over a table of error shapes. It is not a dead export: the packages' pass of the dead-exports check counts a name another file mentions, a test included (Global Constraints). What the fold reads is `failureWordsOf` (Task 9), built on the same tables as `sendFailureWords`.

2. **The Overview reads the lens's three reads to say one fact.** R6 puts `reports_campaigns` in `ROUTE_DRAWS["reports"]` for "drew the most replies". The ledger is the dearest read of the lens, and the Overview is the page most opened. Default: unchanged; narrowing is its own change.
4. **An exact link has no 72-hour bound.** An unsubscribe weeks after an email is the answer to that email's run, so the run sheet and the Home show stops they did not show before. Default: yes, as R4 says "the exact links where a key exists".
5. **One count a person and period, not a count a run.** (Where the code differs, 2.) Default: as ruled, so the lens never exceeds People's.

7. **`campaign_series`' own scan.** Its `sent` CTE reads every recipient of the workspace. Default: leave it unless Task 7 measures it past 4,000 ms at 90,000 recipients; then give it the lead's drive (Task 7, Step 3).
8. **The nightly's workspace holds no failed send.** (Where the code differs, 11.) The failed-send card is proved by the database and fold tests, not by the e2e. If a failed row is wanted there, Today's `send_failures` text is the thing to change first.
9. **Three more figures a later pass could draw:** the share of replies that came from a STOP (a STOP is itself an inbound person's-words message and counts as a reply in every variant: trap, data map §3), `audience.reach`'s left-out people, and a template's quality (GREEN to RED) beside its status. Default: none.
10. **Migration numbers.** 00397 to 00400, renumbered at push only if another lane lands first.
