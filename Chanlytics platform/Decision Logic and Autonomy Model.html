Decision Logic & Autonomy Model

Scope: lead → meeting → verdict lifecycle · auto-send windows · phone-channel rulesDate: Jul 29, 2026

Your instinct draws one clean line: AI owns speed before the meeting; you own judgment after it. Everything below is that line worked out — where it holds, where it needs one more rule, and the nuances that decide whether the product feels unbreakable or spooky.

01The lifecycle and the verdict gate

The meeting is the ownership handoff. Before it, agents optimize response speed. After it, exactly one human decision exists — and it's a closed set, same as the inbox verbs.

AI ownsLead arrives → qualify → nurture → bookrespond < 1 min, any channel · goal is always the appointment
AI ownsAppointment held: reminders, reschedules, no-show recoveryno-show ≠ verdict — agent rebooks per playbook (cap 2 attempts), never asks you
You decideMeeting happened → verdictone tap, three outcomes — surfaces in Inbox the moment the calendar slot ends
Deal→ Pipeline. AI role: admin only (scheduling, docs) — never selling.
Client→ Clients. AI resumes service comms (bookings, reminders) under normal autonomy.
No deal → InterestedAI stands down. Watch-only: inbound from them reopens with full context + bell.
  • Never let a meeting end into silence. Un-verdicted meetings are how CRMs rot. The calendar slot ending fires a verdict request into Inbox (state needs). Unanswered after 48h → auto-files as Interested + one nudge task. Auto-file to the safe outcome, never to Deal.
  • Verdict is one tap, not a form. Three buttons, pre-filled context (transcript sentiment, booking history). AI may suggest ("sounded positive — mark as deal?") but the tap is always yours. That keeps your "no AI after the meeting" principle while AI still runs the interface.
  • Interested is a contract, not a graveyard. Two explicit rules: (1) any inbound from them re-activates qualifying mode instantly, (2) optional owner-set re-permission — "AI may send one check-in after 60/90 days" — off by default. Without rule 2 the bucket silently dies; without "off by default" you break your own boundary.
RecommendationThis slots into the existing enums cleanly: verdict verbs deal | client | interested | archived alongside the decision verbs; lead stage nurture covers Interested with a verdict: "no-deal" reason on the record.

02Three autonomy modes, not two

Your 30-second idea is a genuinely good third mode between ask-first and full auto. Name it and make it a per-agent, per-risk setting instead of a global behavior.

ModeBehaviorWhen
Ask-firstDraft holds until you act. Customer gets the holding message ("checking with the owner — hear back within the hour").Knowledge gaps, escalations, anything money-adjacent — agents never negotiate refunds or discounts; the drafted plan is usually "book them a call with you" (money converts to a meeting). Also the default for new agents earning trust.
Veto windowDraft auto-sends after the window (30s SMS/chat, 15–30 min email) unless you Edit or Hold. Countdown visible in Inbox + bell.Safe replies: confidence ≥ 0.8 and zero committing content. The default for routine conversation.
AutoSends immediately, logged. Shows up in Done, not in your queue.Pure mechanics: confirmations, reminders, FAQ answers — earned via clean-approval milestones.
  • Eligibility is risk-classed, never global. The timer must not be able to auto-send a refund. Rule: inside the agent's authority envelope (Fleet permissions: $ cap, discount cap, booking scope) → veto-window eligible; outside → ask-first, no matter what. This reuses the existing SAFE ≥ 0.8 threshold and Fleet permission model as-is.
  • Be honest about what the window is. With a 30s timer you will miss most windows (asleep, driving, with a client). That's fine — but then it's an opportunity to intervene, not a gate. Design it that way: the message sends on schedule regardless of your presence; the countdown is a courtesy. If you want a real gate, that's ask-first.
  • Any keystroke cancels the timer. Typing in the composer = intent to edit = hold. No explicit "stop" click needed. Escape hatch: a "Hold all" control when 5 countdowns run at once (the burst case — sort the approvals bucket by time-left).
  • The window is the undo. SMS can't be unsent, so the toast pattern inverts: today it's act → toast → Undo; here it's draft → countdown → act. Same single-slot mechanics, mirrored. After send, the record reads "auto-sent after veto window · 28s, no action" — auditable, not mysterious.
  • Per-channel windows. 30s matches SMS/chat social norms. Email's norm is hours — a 15–30 min email window costs nothing and catches far more of your attention windows.
  • The trust dial. Weekly digest: "agents auto-sent 143 replies · you edited 6 (4%)". When the edit rate is low, you widen autonomy with confidence; when it spikes, you tighten. That one number is how the 30s experiment proves itself.

03Phone inverts the model

Correct instinct: you can't hold a live caller for an approval, and you won't be watching the monitor. So phone gets one rule instead of a timer.

The async downgrade rule: a phone agent never decides above its envelope live. It says the holding line ("let me check with the owner — you'll hear back within the hour"), ends gracefully, and the decision becomes a normal text-channel approval in your Inbox. Every over-envelope phone moment deterministically converts into the flow you already have.

  • Warm transfer only when you're reachable. A "Take transfers" toggle (on/off, plus quiet hours) that phone agents check before attempting a hand-to-human; off → holding line. Without presence-awareness, transfers ring into voicemail and burn the lead's goodwill.
  • Hold-and-ask is rightly a future feature. Putting a caller on hold while the agent pings you (30–60s hold, whisper the answer back) is real-time plumbing with ugly failure modes (you don't answer → caller held for nothing → worse than the holding line). Ship the async downgrade first; add hold-and-ask later only for high-value moments, capped at one attempt.
  • Live Monitor is the exception, not the flow. Take Over / End Call / whisper-context stay for the rare watched call — demo-critical, but the product must never depend on you watching.

05What I'd wire into the mockup next

Each of these lands on existing rails — pick any subset.

  • Verdict gate: a completed appointment spawns a verdict thread in Inbox (Deal / Client / Interested as the plan's three outcomes rendered as ONE plan + your tap) + leads:verdict event → Pipeline/Clients/Leads write-through.
  • Veto-window countdown: one Inbox thread in drafts with a live "sending in 24s — Edit · Hold" chip, keystroke-cancels, lands in Done as "auto-sent · no action". Autonomy pane gains the third mode per agent.
  • Take-transfers toggle: topbar presence control; Live Monitor and Fleet cards read it (transfer button disabled with reason when off).
  • Trust digest: one Reports stat row — auto-sent count, edit rate, window-hit rate.

All four reuse the closed verb set, IX_STORE, the event bus, and the gate/toast patterns — no new architecture.
