## Design Context

### Users
Owners and teams of businesses that turn conversations into booked appointments (local shops and clinics, agencies, lenders, online sellers; the first workspace was a hair salon) who run an AI receptionist over the phone, WhatsApp, text and email. They open the dashboard on their phones between clients, often one-handed, for a few seconds at a time. On the Calendar the job is a glance: what is booked today, which times are open, and what the receptionist would offer the next caller. Changing the view, the date or the booking rules is occasional.

### Brand Personality
Sophisticated, minimal, clean, sleek (Ruben's words, 15 Sep 2026). Calm and exact rather than busy: the product does the work, and the page gets out of the way.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Keep the existing system: Geist for UI text, the OKLCH tokens in `apps/dashboard/src/legacy/styles/tokens.css` (hairline `--line` dividers, the 4px spacing scale, the text scale), light and dark themes.
- The working surface comes first. On a phone the first screen shows the thing the page is for (on the Calendar: today's schedule), not a page title, an explanation or a row of stat cards.
- Numbers read as one quiet sentence, not as cards.
- One slim control bar per page on a phone. Explanations, rules and data-source details sit behind a tap or in Settings.
- Anti-references: marketing-style headers with a sub-headline inside a product page; stacked stat cards; several rows of controls before any content; the same title repeated in the top bar and the page.

### Design Principles
1. The work first: the first screen on a phone is the page's content, with chrome kept to a single slim bar.
2. Say it once: nothing the shell already shows (the page title, the status line) is repeated by the page.
3. Numbers as a sentence: counts and next-available facts compress into one line of text.
4. Progressive disclosure: rules, sources and explanations are one tap away, never in the way.
5. Spacing has rhythm: tight inside a group, clear between groups, from the tokens' 4px scale, never ad hoc.
