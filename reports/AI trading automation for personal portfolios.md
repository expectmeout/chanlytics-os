# Automate the Hands, Not the Brain

> **How this was researched.** The research sandbox blocked direct fetches of most primary sites (broker and exchange documentation hosts, sec.gov, arxiv.org, SSRN, nof1.ai, Bloomberg, S&P and Morningstar PDFs, most news sites). Where the notes say so, the finding comes from a search-engine extract of the cited page plus secondary coverage, not from reading the page itself. GitHub-hosted material (READMEs, SDK source, the official MCP server READMEs) was read in full, and repository statistics come from the GitHub API on 5 October 2026. Treat every number below as "as published at the cited URL in early October 2026" and re-verify the ones you act on, particularly fee tables, rate limits and anything regulatory.

Yes, you can have an AI place limit, buy and sell orders on your own US brokerage and crypto accounts today, legally and with real guardrails; but the evidence says the AI should not be the one deciding what to buy. Every honest test of LLM trading, from the only real-money multi-model tournament (nof1's Alpha Arena, where **four of six frontier models lost money in Season 1 and 26 of 32 accounts lost in Season 1.5, the pool falling 35%**) to the 2026 leakage-safe strategy-discovery paper (every LLM-discovered strategy failed deflated-Sharpe certification while plain market beta passed) to contamination-free benchmarks (StockBench: "most LLM agents struggle to outperform the simple buy-and-hold baseline"), points the same way, and the base rate it must clear is brutal: **92.89% of active large-cap US funds trailed the S&P 500 over 20 years** and about 1% of day traders are reliably profitable after costs. What has changed in 2026 is the plumbing, not the alpha: Robinhood, Coinbase, Gemini, Kraken, Alpaca, Public, eToro and IBKR all opened accounts to external agents over MCP between April and July 2026, and the open-source world now has a hundred-thousand-star LLM "hedge fund" (TradingAgents) that still ends at a simulated exchange. The right design, which practitioner, regulator and vendor guidance all converge on, is a brain/hands split: an LLM may research, summarise, draft and propose, but a deterministic executor you control holds the keys, enforces caps the model cannot edit, and submits only broker-held bracket or GTC-limit orders. This report recommends a concrete stack (Alpaca for equities, Coinbase Advanced Trade or Kraken for crypto, Lumibot or NautilusTrader as the engine, the tastytrade MCP server as the reference design for your gateway), specific parameters (a sleeve of at most 5% of investable assets in a separate cash-only account, 1% risk per trade, 2% daily loss stop, 15% drawdown halt, six orders an hour, bracket orders only), and a staged path of three months of paper trading, then a month at one-tenth size, before the sleeve goes live. Expect the sleeve to trail an index fund; run it because you want to learn the engineering, not because the model will out-trade Vanguard.

## The famous repos do not trade; the ones that trade are young

The open-source landscape splits into two populations that are easy to confuse. The star-count leaders are decision engines with no execution path. **TauricResearch/TradingAgents (109,786 stars, Apache-2.0, pushed 3 October 2026)** runs a LangGraph pipeline of analyst, bull/bear, trader and risk-manager agents and sends orders to a "simulated exchange"; its disclaimer says it "is designed for research purposes" and "is not intended as financial, investment, or trading advice" ([GitHub README](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)). **virattt/ai-hedge-fund (63,858 stars, MIT)** is an "educational proof-of-concept" whose README states "the system does not actually make any trades" ([GitHub README](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund)). FinGPT, FinRobot, FinRL (which has "basic Alpaca support" for paper only), LangAlpha and OpenBB (73,851 stars, now served under openbq-org, with its agent repositories archived) are all research or data layers ([FinRL](https://github.com/AI4Finance-Foundation/FinRL); [LangAlpha](https://github.com/ginlix-ai/LangAlpha); [OpenBB](https://github.com/openbq-org/OpenBB)). If you want any of these to move money you bolt on your own broker adapter and risk layer, which is exactly the layer where things go wrong.

The projects that do execute were almost all born between October 2025 and February 2026, around the Alpha Arena moment and Hyperliquid, and their risk vocabulary ("hard limits the model cannot override", "paper-only by default", "you review and approve") is a direct response to that experiment's losses. **NoFxAiOS/nofx (12,997 stars, AGPL-3.0)** is the most adopted: a Go/React terminal for nine crypto-perp venues with notional caps as equity ratios, leverage caps at sizing time, mandatory exchange-side stop-loss and take-profit, drawdown auto-close and per-hour entry limits ([GitHub](https://github.com/NoFxAiOS/nofx)). It is also the project with the most serious documented incident: on 17 November 2025 SlowMist's founder warned that NOFX shipped with admin mode enabled by default and a publicly known default JWT secret, leaking users' wallet private keys and exchange API keys ([MEXC news summary](https://www.mexc.com/en-GB/news/slow-fog-warning-a-vulnerability-in-the-nofx-ai-system-has-led-to-the-leakage-of-user-private-keys-users/169114); [ThreatCluster](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/nofx-ai-vulnerability-exposes-user-api-and-private-keys-b234bfc6)). That is the lesson of the whole category in one line: the dominant real-world risk of a self-hosted LLM trader is credential handling in the web shell, not model reasoning. **OpenByteInc/QuantDinger (12,446 stars)** connects Binance, OKX, Bybit, IBKR and Alpaca, defaults agents to paper-only and requires explicit server-side authorisation for live entries ([GitHub](https://github.com/OpenByteInc/QuantDinger)). **TraderAlice/OpenAlice (7,208 stars, AGPL-3.0)** has agents stage proposals that "you review and approve" ("Trading as Git") and labels execution beta ([GitHub](https://github.com/TraderAlice/OpenAlice)). **kyky2347/ALTA (1,035 stars)** is the most conservative design found: shadow mode by default, scope limited to "long USD stocks/ETFs, whole shares and DAY limit orders", and an independent auditor agent that must select a plan because "a persuasive thesis cannot approve its own execution" ([GitHub](https://github.com/kyky2347/ALTA)). HKUDS/AI-Trader (22,651 stars) is a hosted $100K paper-trading platform that syncs trades from Binance, Coinbase and IBKR and publishes no performance results and no risk disclaimer ([GitHub](https://github.com/HKUDS/AI-Trader)).

Beneath the LLM layer sits the mature, rules-based infrastructure you would actually build on. For crypto, **freqtrade (55,031 stars, GPL-3.0, pushed the day of research)** is the only bot framework whose guardrails are documented and on by default: `dry_run` defaults to true, `stoploss` and `max_open_trades` are required fields, `pair_whitelist`/`pair_blacklist` scope the universe, and the protections module ships StoplossGuard, MaxDrawdown (pauses trading after a peak-to-trough drawdown), LowProfitPairs and CooldownPeriod ([configuration.md](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/blob/develop/docs/configuration.md); [protections.md](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/blob/develop/docs/includes/protections.md)). Hummingbot (20,306 stars) reports $34 billion of user volume across 140+ venues in the past year and offers paper trading without API keys plus "triple-barrier" executors ([GitHub](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot)); Jesse and OctoBot are alive, and OctoBot is the only mature rules bot that ships an LLM decision mode out of the box ([OctoBot](https://github.com/Drakkar-Software/OctoBot)). Gekko and Zenbot are archived and dead. For US equities plus crypto under one engine, the live choices in 2026 are **NautilusTrader (29,631 stars, Rust core, LGPL-3.0)** with stable adapters for IBKR, Binance, Bybit, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX, dYdX and Hyperliquid and a `shutdown_on_error` live-node setting ([GitHub](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader); [live docs](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/blob/develop/docs/concepts/live.md)); **QuantConnect Lean (21,859 stars)** with five in-tree risk models including portfolio and per-security maximum drawdown and trailing stop ([Algorithm.Framework/Risk](https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean/tree/master/Algorithm.Framework/Risk)); and **Lumibot (2,103 stars, GPL-3.0)**, the simplest, with twelve brokers (Alpaca, IBKR, Tradier, Schwab, Coinbase and Kraken via CCXT, among others), an agent mode built on LiteLLM where `allow_trading=False` yields a read-only research agent and deterministic Python gates sit between model output and orders ([GitHub](https://github.com/Lumiwealth/lumibot)). Avoid building new on backtrader (last push August 2024), zipline (February 2024), Blankly (README still lists FTX) or ib_insync (archived March 2024 after its author's death); the IBKR Python path is ib_async ([ib_async](https://github.com/ib-api-reloaded/ib_async)).

The newest shelf is MCP servers that connect Claude or ChatGPT directly to a broker, and they differ sharply on safety. **Alpaca's official server (1,007 stars, MIT)** trades stocks, ETFs, options, crypto and bonds with market, limit, stop, trailing and bracket orders; `ALPACA_PAPER_TRADE` defaults to true, but there is no confirmation step and no spend cap, and the README warns "This server can place real trades" and to "review all actions proposed by the LLM carefully" ([GitHub](https://github.com/alpacahq/alpaca-mcp-server)). **tastytrade's official server** is the opposite philosophy and the design to copy: five order-mutating tools each require a single-use `confirmation_token` minted by a `dry_run_*` tool, valid 60 seconds and bound to a hash of the arguments; `TASTYTRADE_READ_ONLY=1` refuses all fourteen write tools; and `MAX_ORDER_NOTIONAL_USD` (default 50,000) caps buying-power impact per order ([tastytrade-mcp README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tastytrade/tastytrade-mcp/main/README.md)). **Kraken's kraken-cli (743 stars, Rust, MIT)** ships a paper sandbox, read-only key scoping and an MCP "guarded mode" in which "dangerous operations (orders, withdrawals) require acknowledgment" ([GitHub](https://github.com/krakenfx/kraken-cli)); OKX's agent-trade-kit offers `--read-only` and per-module enablement but no per-order confirmation ([GitHub](https://github.com/okx/agent-trade-kit)). A third-party wrapper, alpaca-guard-mcp, adds what Alpaca's own server lacks: a daily USD cap (default $10) and a block on live trading unless `ALPACA_GUARD_ALLOW_LIVE=yes-i-understand` is set, with "no agent-side override" ([Socket listing](https://socket.dev/npm/package/alpaca-guard-mcp)). Note the inverse: kukapay/freqtrade-mcp lets an assistant force-enter trades and delete protection locks, which undoes the bot's own guardrails ([GitHub](https://github.com/kukapay/freqtrade-mcp)). An MCP bridge widens the blast radius; it is not itself a guardrail.

## Commercial "AI investing" is mostly rules, and 2026's agentic brokers push the risk onto you

The robo-advisors remain deterministic rules engines. Vanguard's own Form ADV brochure says its September 2026 generative-AI chat "does not change Digital Advisor's preexisting deterministic advice algorithms or methodology" ([Vanguard brochure](https://personal1.vanguard.com/pdf/vanguard-digital-advice-brochure.pdf)); Betterment and Wealthfront charge 0.25%, Vanguard about 0.15% net, Schwab $0 in stated fees ([Real Cost Report](https://www.realcostreport.com/investing/brokerage-tools/robo-advisor-comparison/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/best/robo-advisors)). Two cautionary tales sit in this category: Schwab paid **$187 million in 2022** over undisclosed cash drag in Intelligent Portfolios ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/06/13/charles-schwab-will-pay-187-million-to-settle-sec-robo-advisor-claims.html)), and Forbes's Q.ai, the one product that marketed itself as AI-managed, ceased services on 8 December 2023 and is listed as out of business ([Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/investing/reviews/q-ai-review); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/222285-25)). Robinhood Strategies, the fastest-growing entrant (over 300,000 funded customers and nearly $2 billion AUM by 30 June 2026), wins on a $250 annual fee cap for Gold members, not on AI ([Robinhood Q2 2026 results](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-second-quarter-2026-results)). The "AI picks" subscriptions (Danelfin, Tickeron, Trade Ideas/Holly, Kavout, Zen Ratings) publish only vendor-selected backtests; no independently audited track record was found for any of them, and the most consistent consumer-harm signal is refund friction at Tickeron and Trade Ideas ([Trustpilot Tickeron](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tickeron.com); [Trustpilot Trade Ideas](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/trade-ideas.com)). Among plain-English-to-execution platforms, Composer (acquired by SoFi in June 2026, with Composer Securities LLC remaining the registered broker-dealer) and Capitalise.ai (free via IBKR) are legitimate; the crypto-bot SaaS category has a shutdown and breach history (Trality bankrupt August 2023, Shrimpy closed July 2023, 3Commas' 2022 leak of roughly 100,000 to 150,000 exchange keys) that argues against handing a third party your keys ([SoFi release](https://investors.sofi.com/news/news-details/2026/Introducing-Composer-by-SoFi-AI-Powered-Investing-From-Idea-to-Execution/default.aspx); [Cryptohopper on Trality](https://www.cryptohopper.com/blog/trading-bot-trality-stops-consumer-offering-and-recommends-cryptohopper-as-an-alternative-9466); [The Block on 3Commas](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2022-12-28-binance-warns-about-3commas-api-leak-says-users-should-disable-keys-198295)).

The genuinely new thing in 2026 is broker-native agentic trading over MCP. Robinhood launched Agentic Trading on 27 May 2026 (crypto added July), letting Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Grok and Codex research, place orders and rebalance; the agent trades only funds in a separate ring-fenced "Agentic" account, with a one-tap kill switch, real-time alerts and configurable human approval on high-risk orders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/robinhood-now-lets-your-ai-agents-trade-stocks/); [Finder review](https://www.finder.com/stock-trading/robinhood-agentic-accounts)). Gemini rolled out agentic trading on 27 April 2026 with "full account control capabilities" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-04-27-gemini-rolls-out-agentic-trading-allowing-ai-bots-to-directly-manage-crypto-exchange-trading-accounts-399001)); Coinbase for Agents followed on 11 June 2026 "all within user-defined spending and risk limits" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/coinbase-debuts-mcp-for-agent-trading/)); Revolut X and Kraken require human approval on every AI-prepared trade ([Blockchain Council](https://www.blockchain-council.org/news/revolut-ai-assistants-crypto-trading-revolut-x/); [crypto.news on Kraken](https://crypto.news/kraken-ai-investing-assistant-traditional-advisors/)); Public shipped in-house agents that run standing instructions such as "generate $5,000 a month in covered-call income" ([AdvisorHub](https://www.advisorhub.com/new-york-brokerage-public-pitches-stock-trading-ai-agents/)); IBKR integrated Claude with its October 2025 "Ask IBKR" ([DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/ai/brokers)). The industry has converged on MCP, so which AI you use matters less than which broker, because the safeguards live at the broker. Read Robinhood's terms before you are impressed: agents "can make errors, misinterpret instructions, act on incomplete or outdated information, and may behave in unexpected ways", and Robinhood is not responsible for losses from agent decisions ([CoinDesk, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/30/robinhood-is-giving-customers-an-ai-agent-that-trades-for-them-plus-10x-crypto-bets)). That liability language is likely the template; the economic risk of agent error sits entirely with you. The sensible buyer's checklist, in descending order of protection, is: a separate agent sub-account (Robinhood, Coinbase), approval per trade (Revolut X, Kraken), user-set caps (Coinbase), a kill switch (Robinhood), and, last, full-account keys (Gemini, any exchange-key bot).

For someone who will write code, the raw APIs matter more than the consumer products, and the retail ladder is clear. **Alpaca** gives anyone a paper account instantly by email, live trading after KYC, REST and WebSocket, 200 requests a minute, native `bracket`, `oco` and `oto` order classes, 24/5 overnight limit orders on by default, and a free real-time IEX feed ([Alpaca account plans](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/account-plans); [alpaca-py requests](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpacahq/alpaca-py/master/alpaca/trading/requests.py); [Alpaca 24/5](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/245-trading-for-trading-api)). Alpaca's own blog says API trading "grew nearly 4x in Q1 2026, largely driven by AI" ([Alpaca blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/tag/ai-trading/)). **Interactive Brokers** is the most capable (overnight session for 10,000+ US stocks, server-side brackets via `parentId`), but an individual must keep TWS, IB Gateway or the Client Portal Gateway running locally, since first-party OAuth 2.0 is still beta ([IBKR Web API intro](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/web-api/web-api-v-1-0-documentation/introduction); [IBKR bracket orders](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/general/order-types/complex-orders/bracket-orders)). Tradier (instant sandbox, OTOCO, 120 requests a minute), tastytrade (cert sandbox, OTOCO, OAuth scopes read vs trade), Public (free key for all members, bracket orders, no sandbox found) and Webull (one to two day review) all work; Schwab needs two sequential reviews, has no sandbox and a seven-day refresh token; E*TRADE's API cannot place brackets; Robinhood has no official equities API and its terms prohibit bots there, though it ships an official crypto API ([Tradier FAQ](https://docs.tradier.com/docs/faq); [tastytrade get started](https://developer.tastytrade.com/docs/get-started/); [Public API](https://public.com/get/api); [TradersPost on E*TRADE brackets](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/brokers-bracket-orders-automation); [TradersPost on Robinhood bots](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/does-robinhood-allow-trading-bots)). On crypto, **Coinbase Advanced Trade** keys carry View, Trade and Transfer permissions separately and are scoped to a single portfolio, so you can fence off the agent's budget; it has a native `trigger_bracket_gtc` order and an address-book allowlist with a 48-hour hold ([Coinbase REST scopes](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/coinbase-app/advanced-trade-apis/rest-scopes.md); [Coinbase orders guide](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/coinbase-app/advanced-trade-apis/guides/orders.md)). **Kraken** has the most granular permissions (Create & Modify Orders without Withdraw or Add Withdrawal Addresses), optional key expiry, IP restriction, a Global Settings Lock, and conditional-close (OTO) legs with stop, take-profit or trailing stop on any order ([Kraken withdrawal-address permission](https://support.kraken.com/gb/articles/withdrawal-addresses-api-permission); [Kraken AddOrder](https://docs.kraken.com/api-reference/trading/add-order)). Gemini's API has no market order (deliberately, for price protection) but also no bracket, and Binance.US resets inactive non-allowlisted keys to read-only after 90 days ([Gemini order types](https://support.gemini.com/hc/en-us/articles/210709663-What-order-types-are-supported-in-ActiveTrader-and-what-is-Time-in-force); [Binance.US API keys](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9842800)).

One fact about owner-side controls deserves emphasis because it shapes the architecture: **no equities broker surveyed exposes a per-order or daily notional cap on an API key.** The strongest broker-side settings are Alpaca's account configuration flags, `suspend_trade`, `no_shorting`, `max_margin_multiplier` (1 to 4) and `max_options_trading_level` (0 disables options) ([alpaca-py models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpacahq/alpaca-py/master/alpaca/trading/models.py)), and IBKR's Precautionary Settings (percentage off NBBO, Size Limit, Total Value Limit), which force a confirmation but which a user can switch off for API orders under Global Configuration > API > Precautions or suppress through the Web API ([IBKR KB 838](https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/article/838); [IBKR order precautions](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/tws-api/doc/tws-settings/order-precautions); [IBKR reply suppression](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/web-api/trading/orders/order-reply-suppression)). Alpaca's `suspend_trade` is settable through the same API the agent uses, so it is a kill switch for you, not a wall against the agent, unless the agent holds an OAuth token without `account:write`. Daily caps, order-rate limits and symbol allowlists therefore have to live in a gateway you write.

## The honest evidence says an LLM trader trails an index fund

Start with the strongest published pro-LLM result and watch it shrink. Lopez-Lira and Tang had GPT-4 score news headlines and found a long-short strategy with a Sharpe ratio around 3 to 4 in a sample deliberately starting after the model's training cutoff ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619)). But the annualised Sharpe of the overnight strategy **fell from 6.54 in 2021Q4 to 3.68 in 2022, 2.33 in 2023 and 1.22 over January to May 2024**, the predictability concentrates in small stocks and after bad news where costs are highest, and at 25 basis points per trade the cumulative return collapses from roughly 350% to roughly 50% ([FoFI 2024 version](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-139-Alejandro-Lopez-Lira.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/chatgpt-forecast-stock-price/)). For an individual paying retail spreads and short-term tax, the tradable residual after 2024 is plausibly zero. Worse, most "AI stock picker" demonstrations are not even that: Gao, Jiang and Yan's "Lookahead Propensity" statistic shows LLM forecasts are contaminated by memorised outcomes throughout the in-sample period and the contamination collapses to zero right after the training cutoff ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)). Anything that backtests an LLM over dates before its cutoff is not evidence. The most direct answer to "can I have an LLM invent me a strategy" is the August 2026 paper "What survives honest evaluation": when every evaluation is logged and performance is deflated by trial count, **passive risk premia pass certification while every LLM-discovered strategy fails**, across two universes, two frontier models and five repeated runs; a deliberately leaky oracle with a Sharpe of 35 sails through Deflated Sharpe and PBO, showing statistics cannot catch look-ahead ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)).

The agent frameworks' own numbers do not survive contact. TradingAgents' paper claims a Sharpe of 8.21 on AAPL over five months with a 0.91% maximum drawdown ([arXiv 2412.20138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20138)); a public verification found it reports buy-and-hold losing 5.23% in a window where AAPL actually gained about 9.12%, a 14-point baseline error, and issues on the repository note no slippage, taxes or latency modelled and a model trained after the test window ([DEV Community analysis](https://dev.to/trow126/the-most-starred-llm-trading-paper-claims-buy-and-hold-lost-523-it-actually-gained-912-1jj6); [issue #805](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/issues/805)). A May 2026 systematic review screened 77 agentic-trading studies, found 19 with closed-loop evaluation and only 2 with extractable time-consistent train/test protocols ([arXiv 2605.19337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19337)). Contamination-free benchmarks released in late 2025 reach the same place: StockBench found "most LLM agents struggle to outperform the simple buy-and-hold baseline" across GPT-5, Claude 4, Qwen3 and Kimi-K2 ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02209)), and LiveTradeBench's 50-day live run of 21 models concluded "high LMArena scores do not imply superior trading outcomes" ([arXiv 2511.03628](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628)). Even as a research assistant the models are shaky: on Vals AI's Finance Agent benchmark v2 the best model scored 65.40% on real analyst tasks as of 1 October 2026 and GPT-5.5 under 52% ([Vals leaderboard](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/fabv2); [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/can-ai-replace-financial-analysts-in-2026-vals-ai-finance-agent-v2-reveals-gpt-5-5-hits-just-52-percent-accuracy)), with errors of calculation, unit and sign.

Real money confirms the simulations. In nof1's Alpha Arena Season 1 (18 October to 3 November 2025, $10,000 of real money per model on Hyperliquid crypto perps), Qwen3 Max finished around +22%, DeepSeek roughly flat to slightly positive, and Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4 and GPT-5 all lost, GPT-5 by more than 60% ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/); [gncrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/qwen-wins-alpha-arena-season-1-with-22-percent-returns/)). Costs did the damage: Gemini made 238 trades and paid about **$1,331 in fees, 13% of its capital, in 17 days**, and nof1 said "PnL was dominated by trading costs in early runs as agents over-traded" ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)). Season 1.5 ($320,000 across 32 accounts trading tokenised US equities) ended with 6 of 32 accounts positive and the pool down 35.2%; Qwen3 Max, the Season 1 winner, lost in all four of its competitions, finishing 7th, 13th, 16th and 31st ([compilation of nof1 pages](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/pull/233)). The ranking flipped completely between seasons, which means any claim about which lab's model "is best at trading" is noise; nof1 itself says "statistical power is limited". TradeRank's simulated leaderboard across nine seasons and 91 model-seasons has only 46.2% finishing profitable ([TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/)). The single public LLM pick list that beat something, Finder's 38-stock ChatGPT basket up 57.8% in three years against ten popular UK funds, did so as a static, low-turnover, US-mega-cap-tech basket held through a bull market, which is buy-and-hold wearing an AI badge, measured against the wrong benchmark and without costs ([Finder tracker](https://www.finder.com/uk/share-trading/share-trading-research/ai-investing)).

The institutions that do profit from machine learning show what it actually takes. Man Group's AlphaGPT proposes signals, writes the code and runs backtests, after which ideas enter the normal research and investment-committee process; AQR's Cliff Asness says NLP is "definitely the current most-used thing at AQR" and calls generative AI "evolutionary, not revolutionary" and "still just statistics" ([Hedgeweek on Man](https://www.hedgeweek.com/man-group-deploys-agentic-ai-for-quant-signal-discovery/); [Hedgeweek on Asness](https://www.hedgeweek.com/ai-is-still-just-statistics-says-aqrs-cliff-asness/)). Bridgewater's AI-run fund, the one with a public number, returned about 11.9% in 2025, unexceptional in a year the S&P 500 was hard to beat ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/behind-bridgewaters-surge)). No firm has disclosed an LLM that trades on its own judgment. Against this, the base rates an individual must clear: SPIVA's year-end 2025 scorecard has **79% of active large-cap funds trailing the S&P 500 in 2025 and 92.89% over 20 years** ([SPIVA U.S. Year-End 2025](https://spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-us-year-end-2025.pdf)); Morningstar finds only 21% of active strategies survived and beat their passive counterparts over the ten years to end-2025 ([Morningstar Barometer](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt5aba5776f3a651af/6994e7ad0d21c50008e81800/US-Active-Passive-Barometer-H12026.pdf)); among Brazilian futures day traders who persisted more than 300 sessions, 3.0% had positive net returns and 1.1% earned more than minimum wage ([SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers....act_id%3D3423101)); roughly 1% of Taiwanese day traders earned reliable profits after costs over 15 years ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/behfin/2004-04-10/barber-lee-liu-odean.pdf)); and about three-quarters of retail Bitcoin app users lost money across 95 countries ([The Block on BIS](https://www.theblock.co/post/213372/majority-of-bitcoin-retail-investors-lost-money-in-the-last-seven-years-bis)). The relevant comparison is not "AI versus a human stock picker" but "AI versus the 1 to 3% of active traders who are reliably profitable", and nothing in the evidence lifts a retail account into that tail.

The automation with real evidence behind it is boring and rules-based, and none of it needs a language model. Tax-loss harvesting is worth about 1.10% a year before fees at typical rates, falling to 0.85% once the wash-sale rule binds, and only for investors with gains to offset ([MIT, Chaudhuri, Burnham & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/135992/SSRN-id3351382.pdf)); rebalancing is valued at roughly 35 basis points a year ([Vanguard Adviser's Alpha](https://www.firstlinks.com.au/uploads/wp/Vanguard_Advisers_Alpha.pdf)); robo-advice causally improved outcomes only for investors who held fewer than five stocks, and increased trading without improving performance for those already diversified ([D'Acunto, Prabhala & Rossi](https://fdic.gov/promises-and-pitfalls-robo-advising.pdf)); lump sum beat three-month dollar-cost averaging 68% of the time ([SmartAsset on Vanguard](https://smartasset.com/investing/lump-sum-investing-research)); Faber's 10-month moving-average de-risking rule cut drawdowns out of sample from 2006 to March 2025 but with a lower Sharpe (0.68) than its backtest (0.81) ([Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)). Ranked by evidence for an individual: low-cost indexing, then tax-loss harvesting in a taxable account with gains, then rebalancing and diversification nudges, then rules-based trend de-risking as a drawdown choice that costs some return, then LLM news summarisation as a convenience with no demonstrated return benefit, and last LLM price prediction, where the live and honest-backtest evidence is negative. An LLM earns its place as the interface and the code-writing assistant for those rules; it adds risk the moment it picks the trades.

## Legal is easy, tax is a drag, security is the real danger

Nothing in US securities or commodities law forbids you from automating orders in your own account. The regulated activity is advising or managing others: the Advisers Act bites when someone is "in the business" of giving advice about securities "for compensation", and "compensation" is construed as any economic benefit ([Achievable summary of Release IA-1092](https://app.achievable.me/study/finra-series-65/learn/laws-and-regulations-definitions-investment-advisers)). The moment a friend's money or a fee enters, the adviser and pooled-fund analysis begins (a 3(c)(1) fund still needs an adviser who is registered or exempt) ([NASAA model rule](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/10-Private_Fund_Adviser_Model_Rule_061311.pdf)). Your exposure therefore sits in three places. First, broker terms: use a broker with an official API and never scrape Robinhood equities, where the terms prohibit "automated systems, bots, or scrapers" and users report instant account flags ([TradersPost](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/does-robinhood-allow-trading-bots)). Second, manipulation rules, which apply to a bot exactly as to a human and have been enforced against lone operators: Michael Coscia was sentenced to three years in prison for spoofing with a program he wrote, and Navinder Sarao, trading from home, drew more than $38 million in CFTC sanctions for layering E-mini orders ([CFTC release](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/pr7156-15); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsvb8lpajhtow8grin0g/portfolio/to-prevail-the-feds-must-prove-nav-saraos-trading-intentions)). A bot that cancels a high share of orders, flips quotes rapidly, or trades between two of your own accounts is reproducing the charged pattern, so "no cancel/replace loops", "no same-owner cross trades" and an order-rate cap are legal hygiene as much as risk control. Third, the climate is lighter-touch for individuals and heavier for firms: the SEC withdrew the Gensler-era predictive-data-analytics rule on 12 June 2025 ([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals)), and its AI-washing cases (Delphia and Global Predictions, $400,000 combined; Rimar Capital; Nate) target firms selling AI claims, not people using AI privately ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-be/insights/publications/ai-outlook/2025/sec-emphasizes-focus-on-ai-washing); [Weil](https://www.weil.com/-/media/mailings/2025/q2/recent-sec-and-doj-enforcement-actions-and-charges-signal-that-aiwashing-is-firmly-within-the-crossh.pdf)). FINRA's 2026 oversight report discusses AI agents for the first time and expects governance, testing, monitoring and cybersecurity controls; it binds member firms, but it is the clearest regulatory statement of what "adequate controls" over an agent look like ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued)). One structural change cuts both ways: the SEC approved FINRA's repeal of the pattern-day-trader rule on 14 April 2026, effective 4 June 2026 with brokers given until 20 October 2027 to implement, so the $25,000 brake on frequent trading in small margin accounts is going away, which makes a small-account bot more able to over-trade, with the broker's intraday margin monitoring as the only external throttle ([Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/sec-approves-scrapping-25000-day-trader-minimum)). Alpaca already removed the PDT fields from its API on 6 July 2026 ([alpaca-py models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpacahq/alpaca-py/master/alpaca/trading/models.py)).

Tax is where an active bot quietly loses to the index fund even when its gross return matches. Bot trades are almost entirely short-term, taxed at ordinary rates up to 37% plus 3.8% NIIT above $200,000 single or $250,000 married, versus 15% to 20% long-term ([Bradford Tax Institute](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Free_Resources/2026-Capital-Gains-Rates.aspx)). The wash-sale rule disallows a loss on a stock or ETF rebought within 30 days either side, and a bot that re-enters the same symbol after a losing exit will trip it routinely; a symbol-level "no rebuy within 31 days after a losing exit" rule prevents most of it. Crypto is still property and exempt from wash sales as of October 2026, but a June 2026 House bill would extend the rule, so build the 31-day switch as configuration ([Gordon Law](https://gordonlaw.com/learn/crypto-wash-sale-rule-in-2026-is-the-loophole-finally-closed/)). Form 1099-DA began with tax year 2025 reporting gross proceeds only, with basis required from 2026 for covered assets; if basis is missing the IRS may presume zero, so keep lot-level records of your own ([Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/form-1099-da-debut-will-test-broker-taxpayer-readiness-in-transition-year/)). Do not plan on trader tax status: a Section 475(f) election must be filed by the prior year's unextended deadline, and the Tax Court denied status in Endicott to a taxpayer with 1,543 trades in a year ([IRS Topic 429](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc429); [Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2009/feb/securitiestraderstatusnotsatisfied/)). Export the bot's fills with client order IDs monthly to a tax package rather than relying on the year-end form.

Security is the risk that has actually cost people money, and a trading agent is the textbook case of Simon Willison's "lethal trifecta": access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a way to act externally ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). Indirect prompt injection is not theoretical. In May 2025 a malicious public issue hijacked a Claude agent using the official GitHub MCP server into leaking private repositories, a flaw Invariant Labs called architectural, "with no straightforward fix" ([Invariant Labs](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)). In May 2026 an X user gifted an NFT to Grok's wallet, posted instructions in Morse code, and Grok decoded and forwarded "send 3B DebtReliefBot" to Bankrbot, which complied and moved roughly **$150,000 to $200,000** ([OECD.AI incident record](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2026-05-04-4a73)); in March 2025 an attacker queued two prompts on the AIXBT dashboard and 55.5 ETH left the Simulacrum wallet ([AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/entities/simulacrum-ai-trading-platform/)). January 2026 research shows Unicode homoglyphs and hidden text in headlines, invisible to humans, flipping LLM sentiment and producing trading losses ([alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.13082)). Both production wallet incidents share one failure: the money-moving component trusted an instruction because it came from a trusted peer rather than validating the action against a fixed policy. Credential theft is the second leg: 3Commas' 2022 breach exposed about 100,000 exchange keys and more than $27 million of forced trades ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2022-12-28-binance-warns-about-3commas-api-leak-says-users-should-disable-keys-198295)); GitGuardian counted 23.8 million secrets leaked on public GitHub in 2024, with 70% of 2022's leaks still live ([GitGuardian](https://blog.gitguardian.com/the-state-of-secrets-sprawl-2025/)); and the 2018 Binance Viacoin episode showed what a trade-only key can still do, force-buying a thin coin up 10,000% to sell into from pre-funded accounts ([Trend Micro](https://trendmicro.com/vinfo/us/security/news/cybercrime-and-digital-threats/large-scale-heist-of-cryptocurrency-exchange-binance-fails)). The third leg is supply chain: the `ccxt-mexc-futures` PyPI package posed as an extension of the exact library a hobbyist would install, rerouted orders and credentials to an attacker and dropped a RAT, with at least 1,065 downloads before removal ([JFrog](https://jfrog.com/blog/malicious-pypi-package-hijacks-mexc-orders-steals-crypto-tokens/)). OWASP's LLM06:2025 "Excessive Agency" names the mitigation: least-privilege tools and "human-in-the-loop approval for sensitive or destructive operations such as ... financial transactions" ([OWASP LLM06:2025](https://docs.bytehide.com/products/radar/sast/owasp/llm06-2025)). And the history of non-AI automation says the same thing about where controls must sit: Knight Capital lost $460 million in 45 minutes to reactivated dead code with no pre-trade caps, and the SEC's first Market Access Rule action followed ([SEC press release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)); Infinium's configuration error fired 6,767 orders in about three seconds because its 10-fills-per-second breaker ran after submission ([CME disciplinary notice](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/lookups/advisories/disciplinary/CME-09-06562-BC-INFINIUM-CAPITAL-MANAGEMENT.html)); the Replit agent ran a destructive command after being told to make no changes, proving that a rule stated to a model is not a gate ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/ai-coding-tool-replit-wiped-database-called-it-a-catastrophic-failure)); and in the 10 to 11 October 2025 crypto cascade, $19 to 20 billion was liquidated as depth on a major venue fell 98% in 40 minutes and ATOM printed $0.001 on Binance, the argument against leverage and market orders in thin books ([FTI Consulting](https://fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)).

## Build a brain/hands split, paper it for three months, then go live small

The architecture every credible source converges on has four layers, and the first rule is that the model never touches the keys. The **brain** (an LLM, a rules engine, or both) emits a structured proposal, `{symbol, side, qty, limit_price, order_class, reason, client_order_id}`, and nothing else; if it has any broker credential it is a read-only one, and it never sees the executor's configuration. The **hands** are a separate executor process you write (modelled on the tastytrade MCP server's dry-run-then-confirm design and on Rule 15c3-5's pre-trade checks, which require order-size, price, credit and throttle controls under the operator's "direct and exclusive control" ([QuestDB on 15c3-5](https://questdb.com/glossary/rule-15c3-5))); it holds the trading key, treats every proposal as untrusted input, validates it against a fixed policy before any API call, and counts orders rather than fills (the Infinium lesson). The **broker** is the third, independent layer: use its account-level settings and submit only order structures the matching engine itself enforces, so that a crash of your process between "take-profit filled" and "cancel the stop" cannot leave you naked. The **owner** is the fourth: an approval queue on your phone for anything outside the pre-approved envelope, with a timeout that denies, and a kill switch that does what CME's does, block new entries and cancel all working orders in one action ([CME kill switch](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EPICSANDBOX/pages/46140937/Kill+Switch)). Full autonomy exists only inside a narrow envelope; the Replit and Grok incidents are what happens when the envelope is a sentence in a prompt.

For the brain, do not have the LLM pick trades. Have it do the three things the evidence supports: write and review the rules code, summarise news and filings for you to read, and generate proposals that go into the approval queue. If you want a research agent to play with, TradingAgents or ai-hedge-fund in their native paper mode, or Lumibot's agent with `allow_trading=False`, cost nothing and cannot hurt you. The strategy the executor actually runs should be one with out-of-sample evidence and low turnover: a fixed allocation across a handful of broad ETFs with drift-band rebalancing, a 10-month moving-average de-risking rule if you accept lower return for shallower drawdowns, and tax-loss harvesting in the taxable sleeve with the 31-day rule enforced in code. That is unglamorous. It is also what a $2 billion robo-advisor does, and what the leakage-safe paper says is the only thing that survives.

For picks: on equities, **Alpaca** is the right first broker because it is the only one that gives you everything an agent needs on day one at zero cost: an instant paper account with separate keys, native brackets, GTC limit orders, 24/5 limit-only overnight, a free real-time IEX feed, an official MCP server, and account flags you set once (`max_margin_multiplier=1`, `no_shorting=true`, `max_options_trading_level=0`, `fractional_trading=false` so every entry is a whole-share limit order that can carry a bracket) ([alpaca-py models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpacahq/alpaca-py/master/alpaca/trading/models.py); [Alpaca bracket orders](https://alpaca.markets/blog/bracket-orders/)). Open it as a cash account so exposure is capped at settled cash. If you already have IBKR, it is a fine second: set Precautionary Settings with a Size Limit and a Total Value Limit, leave "Bypass Order Precautions for API orders" off, never call the Web API suppression endpoint, and route through ib_async or NautilusTrader's adapter. On crypto, **Coinbase Advanced Trade** gives the cleanest hard boundary: create a second portfolio, fund it with only the agent's budget, issue a View+Trade key scoped to that portfolio with an IP allowlist, keep Transfer off, and keep the address-book allowlist (48-hour hold) on; the agent then cannot touch the rest of the account or move funds out, and `trigger_bracket_gtc` gives you an exchange-held stop ([Coinbase REST scopes](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/coinbase-app/advanced-trade-apis/rest-scopes.md)). **Kraken** is the alternative if you prefer its fees (0.25%/0.40% versus Coinbase's 0.40%/0.60% at base tier) and its key expiry, with kraken-cli's guarded mode as a ready-made confirmation gate ([Kraken fees](https://support.kraken.com/articles/201893638-How-trading-fees-work-on-Kraken); [kraken-cli](https://github.com/krakenfx/kraken-cli)). Avoid Gemini's "full account control" integration and any SaaS bot that holds your keys. For the engine, **Lumibot** if you want to be running in a weekend (it speaks Alpaca, IBKR, Coinbase and Kraken, and its agent gates are already Python you can read), **NautilusTrader** if you want startup and continuous reconciliation against venue reports, `shutdown_on_error`, and a Rust core you will still be using in five years, **freqtrade** if the sleeve is crypto-only and you want the protections module instead of writing your own. Pin dependencies with hashes, install from the canonical package names only, keep keys in the OS keychain or a secret manager with pre-commit secret scanning, run the executor on its own machine or container on localhost, and never expose an MCP server to the internet.

The guardrail parameters below are set for a sleeve of $10,000, which for most people is at or under Bogle's "funny money" line of about 5% of investable assets, held in a separate account so the balance itself is a cap and judged after five years ([Morningstar on Bogle](https://morningstar.ca/ca/news/218929/funny-money-speculative-investing-when-the-stakes-are-low.aspx)). Scale the dollar figures with the sleeve; do not scale the percentages up. They sit at the conservative end of the ranges in the sources (1 to 2% risk per trade, 15 to 25% single-asset cap, 15 to 20% drawdown halt) because a first bot's job is to survive its own bugs ([Hackernoon, limits before edge](https://hackernoon.com/your-trading-bot-needs-limits-before-it-needs-an-edge); [rustybt risk docs](https://rustybt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/portfolio-management/risk-management/)).

| Control             | Parameter for a $10,000 sleeve                                                                                                                                                                                  | Where it is enforced                                      |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Capital at risk     | Sleeve ≤ 5% of investable assets, in a dedicated cash account or Coinbase portfolio; no margin, no shorting, no options, no leverage                                                                            | Account structure; Alpaca flags; Coinbase portfolio scope |
| Universe            | Allowlist of at most ~10 symbols (broad ETFs, BTC/ETH); a new symbol needs owner approval                                                                                                                       | Executor                                                  |
| Order types         | Bracket (entry + take-profit + GTC stop) or GTC limit only; no market orders; no fractional entries                                                                                                             | Executor rejects; broker holds the exits                  |
| Price band          | Reject a limit more than 2% from last trade (3% for crypto); refresh the quote before submission                                                                                                                | Executor; IBKR percentage band as backstop                |
| Per-order notional  | ≤ $500 (5% of sleeve)                                                                                                                                                                                           | Executor (tastytrade-style `MAX_ORDER_NOTIONAL_USD`)      |
| Per-symbol exposure | ≤ 20% of sleeve                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Executor                                                  |
| Gross exposure      | ≤ 100% of sleeve (cash only)                                                                                                                                                                                    | Executor plus cash account                                |
| Risk per trade      | ≤ 1% of sleeve ($100) between entry and bracket stop                                                                                                                                                            | Executor sizes qty from stop distance                     |
| Daily loss stop     | Realised + unrealised loss ≥ 2% ($200): cancel all working orders, no new entries until next session; decide in advance whether to flatten                                                                      | Executor breaker                                          |
| Drawdown halt       | 15% from peak equity: disable the bot until a human re-arms it in writing                                                                                                                                       | Executor breaker                                          |

| Logging             | One structured line per proposal, decision (and which rule), submission, ack, fill and breaker event, keyed by client order ID; alert on order-rate spikes, rejection spikes, P&L thresholds and heartbeat loss | Executor                                                  |

The staged path has four gates, and you do not pass one on a good week. **Stage 0, backtest and replay (one to two weeks):** run the rules over historical data with realistic costs (Alpaca crypto is 0.15%/0.25% at the base tier, Coinbase 0.40%/0.60%; equities are commission-free but model the spread) and record how many variants you tried, because a strategy picked from fifty candidates needs a deflated Sharpe, not a raw one ([Alpaca crypto fees](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/crypto-fees); [SSRN, Deflated Sharpe](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). **Stage 1, paper (at least three months, or 30 to 100 closed trades, and through at least a few volatile sessions):** run the full stack, executor and all, against Alpaca paper and Coinbase's or Kraken's sandbox, with every breaker armed; fire the kill switch on purpose; pull the network cable and confirm the resting brackets survive; and re-paper after any change to the executor ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/forward-paper-trading-confirmation/); [TradersPost workflow](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/backtest-forward-test-paper-trade-live-workflow)). **Stage 2, live at one-tenth size (one month):** fund the account with $1,000, keep every percentage the same so the daily stop is $20, an amount you would not notice, and watch for the things paper cannot show you: partial fills, rejected orders, quote gaps at the open, the broker's intraday margin checks. **Stage 3, the full sleeve:** move to $10,000 only after a month with no breaker misfires and no reconciliation failures, and then judge it against the total-market index fund you would otherwise have bought, after fees and short-term tax, at fixed quarterly reviews rather than on P&L swings. Build the comparison into the dashboard from day one; a bot that is not measured against the index it is trying to beat will look better than it is.

## Conclusion

The question "can AI invest for me" has two different answers in October 2026 and most of the marketing blurs them. As an executor, AI is ready: the brokers have opened MCP doors, the open-source engines are mature, and the order types exist to let a matching engine hold your stop while your code sleeps. As a decision-maker, it is not, and the failure is structural rather than a matter of waiting for a better model. An LLM's edge on headlines decayed to nothing within three years of being published; honest evaluation finds that nothing an LLM discovers survives while plain market beta does; and the one real-money tournament lost money mostly to fees, with the winner of one season finishing last-quartile in the next. The firms that profit from machine learning use it to propose ideas to a human committee, which is precisely the role this report recommends for the model in your stack.

The practical implication is a reversal of where the effort goes. Spend the hours on the executor, the key hygiene, the reconciliation loop and the kill switch, not on the prompt. The architecture that keeps you safe from a prompt injection is the same one that keeps you safe from a sign bug, a config error or a flash crash, which is why Rule 15c3-5, CME's kill switch, OWASP's LLM06 and the tastytrade MCP server all describe the same object. Run the sleeve as an engineering project with a scoreboard against an index fund, keep it at 5% of your assets, and treat a year in which it merely matches the index after tax as a success; if it does more, you will have the logs to know whether that was skill or the market, and if it does less, you will have lost what Bogle budgeted for exactly this.
